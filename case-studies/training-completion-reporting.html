Case Study 03 · Reporting & Process Improvement

Training Completion Visibility & WFM Reporting

Designing a practical reporting approach that connects changing roster data, training status, completion, and priority so WFM and operations leaders can see readiness gaps clearly.

WFM ReportingExcel Data ManagementOperational Readiness
Situation

The roster is not static.

Training activity creates a WFM planning challenge because agents can be added, removed, moved between groups, or change training status while a reporting period is in progress. A useful report therefore needs to connect training information with the current roster rather than relying on a static list.

Challenge

What the report needed to show

  • Current training population
  • Training type and name
  • Start and end dates
  • Completion percentage and status
  • Priority and outstanding requirements
  • Management-level readiness visibility
Reporting approach

Make changing workforce data usable.

01

Structure the source information

Organize training information around operationally useful fields: training type, training name, LOB, number of users, start date, end date, completion percentage, completion status, and priority.

02

Account for roster movement

Design the tracker around a changing roster so current users, completed users, and outstanding requirements can be identified without repeated manual rebuilding.

03

Separate completion from priority

Completion answers where training stands. Priority answers how urgently it needs attention. Keeping them separate creates a clearer management view.

04

Create management-level visibility

Surface overall completion, training populations, outstanding users, status by category, priority items, and dates while keeping detailed records available for follow-up and auditability.

Process logic

From roster to action.

Current Roster→Training Population→Training Status→Completion %→Priority Classification→Management Summary→Follow-Up / Action
Business value

Better visibility into readiness.

The reporting approach reduces dependence on manually maintained static lists and gives WFM and operational leaders a structured way to identify completion gaps, prioritize follow-up, and understand how training activity interacts with the current workforce.
