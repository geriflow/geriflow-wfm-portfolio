# Case Study 02 — Intraday Performance Management & Queue Stabilization

**Role:** Workforce Management / Real-Time Operations  
**Scope:** Intraday monitoring, queue management, staffing interventions  

> **Confidentiality note:** Client names, site identifiers, proprietary system details, and sensitive operational data are intentionally omitted. This case study focuses on the WFM decision framework and operational methodology.

## Situation

In a large contact center environment, intraday conditions could shift quickly as call demand, staffing availability, adherence, and queue performance changed throughout the day.

The WFM challenge was not simply to report what happened. It was to identify where capacity or performance risk was developing and translate that information into timely operational action.

## WFM Challenge

- Multiple queues competing for available capacity
- Changing staffing conditions throughout the day
- Service-level risk when demand exceeded available capacity
- Need for fast communication with Operations and leadership
- Need to balance customer impact with operational constraints

## Response

### 1. Monitor real-time queue conditions

Reviewed queue conditions and intraday performance indicators to identify where customer impact was developing.

Key signals included:

- Average Speed of Answer (ASA)
- Calls waiting
- Abandon rate
- Service level
- Agent availability
- Adherence and staffing conditions

### 2. Prioritize the highest-impact queues

Rather than distributing available capacity evenly, interventions were prioritized toward queues showing the strongest combination of immediate demand and customer impact.

This created a practical decision sequence:

**Queue pressure → Capacity gap → Available staffing → Operational intervention → Recheck results**

### 3. Coordinate staffing actions

When additional capacity was available, staffing actions were coordinated with the appropriate operational leaders. Depending on the situation, interventions could include moving eligible cross-trained capacity, adjusting activities, optimizing intraday schedules, or requesting additional support.

### 4. Maintain continuous communication

Real-time updates were shared with stakeholders and leadership so that operational teams had visibility into queue conditions, actions being taken, and whether interventions were improving performance.

### 5. Reassess and adjust

WFM decisions were treated as iterative rather than one-time actions. Queue conditions were reviewed again after interventions so that capacity could be redirected if the highest-priority risk changed.

## Decision Framework

```text
Real-Time Data
      ↓
Identify Queue Risk
      ↓
Assess Available Capacity
      ↓
Validate Eligible Staffing
      ↓
Apply Intervention
      ↓
Monitor Impact
      ↓
Reallocate / Escalate if Needed
```

## Business Value

This approach positioned WFM as an active operational decision function rather than a reporting-only function. By continuously connecting queue conditions to available capacity and operational actions, the team could respond faster to changing intraday risk and keep customer-impact measures visible to leadership.
